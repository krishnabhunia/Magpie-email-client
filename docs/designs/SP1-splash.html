Option AQuiet markSP1-A

Just the mark, a name and one line of progress

A small borderless card in the middle of the screen. It follows Magpie's theme (Match Windows / Light / Dark), shows the version, and says what Magpie is doing in one line. Closes with a short fade the moment the main window has drawn.

440 × 270 px
Status lines are the real start-up stages in App.OnStartup.

Good

  • Calm, matches the Spark-style quiet look Magpie already has
  • Smallest change; nothing new to learn
  • Reads well in both themes

Trade-offs

  • If start-up is slow you only see one line
  • Nothing special after an update (the tray balloon still says "Magpie updated")

Status lines it cycles through

  • Checking the last update…
  • Opening your mail folder…
  • Loading your settings…
  • Starting 3 accounts…
Option BStart-up stepsSP1-B

Show each start-up step with its time

A dark brand strip on the left; on the right, the four things Magpie really does before the window opens, ticked off as they finish, with milliseconds. Your accounts appear as chips in their own colour dots. Useful for seeing where a slow start comes from.

Good

  • Honest: shows exactly what Magpie is waiting on
  • A slow step (locked drive, big mail folder move) is obvious
  • Account chips confirm every account came up

Trade-offs

  • Busier; on a fast PC the steps flash past (kept at least 0.8 s)
  • Shows email addresses on screen at start (a Settings switch can hide them)

If a step fails

The step turns red and the card shows the message with Open log folder and, after an update, Go back to the previous version, in place of today's plain Windows message box.

Option CPlume + What's newSP1-C

A branded card that turns into "What's new" after an update

Always dark ink with a slow drifting plume of teal and purple feather strokes (the colours of the icon). On a normal start it shows the name and progress. The first start after an update adds the version jump and the top 3 lines from the CHANGELOG, then carries on by itself.

Good

  • Most distinctive; feels like a finished product
  • Tells you what changed after an update, at the moment you notice the update
  • "Continue" counts down 4 s, so it never blocks

Trade-offs

  • Always dark, does not follow the Light theme
  • The plume animation costs a little GPU for ~1 s (off with Windows "Animation effects" off)
  • Largest change of the three

Where "What's new" comes from

The release notes the updater already downloads from the GitHub release (first 3 bullet headlines). Offline or none found: the card shows only the version jump.

When the splash appears, and how you turn it off

The splash runs on its own thread, so it keeps moving while Magpie opens its data folder and database on the main thread. It appears within ~150 ms of double-clicking.
